# Cascade Mountain (Portage, Wisconsin): pay, hours, housing and deductions for J-1 Work and Travel winter participants

Research date: 2026-10-19. Method caveat: in this research environment, direct page fetching (WebFetch, curl, scraping tools, Wayback Machine) was blocked by DNS or proxy errors. Every finding below comes from search-engine result summaries and snippets of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Treat exact figures as "reported by the cited page per search snippet" and re-check on the live page before publishing. Many job-board figures carry no posting date.

## 1. Hourly wages by position (2023-24 to 2026-27), and whether any role is tipped

### Takeaway
Recent Cascade Mountain postings put most entry-level roles that a J-1 student would get (lift operator, food and beverage, retail/shop, park crew, ski school) at about **$14–$17/hour**. Snowmaking is $18–$19, leads and supervisors are $17–$25, and maintenance technicians are $23–$40. The resort's own homepage, current at the time of the search (2026-27 hiring), advertises "starting wages as high as $19/hour, no experience needed." Bartender, listed at $12/hour, is the only role that looks tipped.

- The rates above sit far above Wisconsin's $7.25 minimum. They reflect a tight local labor market rather than any legal floor.
- No 2023-24 rate could be tied to a confirmed date, so the change in pay from season to season could not be measured.
- Bartender at $12/hour plus tips is the only role that looks tipped. Whether J-1 students are placed in bar roles, and what the age and licensing rules are for serving alcohol, was not researched here. Treat tips as zero for J-1 estimates.

## 2. Weekly hours, weather and holiday effects, and overtime

### Takeaway
No source states how many hours per week J-1 students get at Cascade. Long hours are plausible: the resort is open until 9 p.m. on weekdays and 10 p.m. on weekends, and holidays are mandatory. No reports of hours being cut in warm winters were found, and the resort has 100% snowmaking coverage. Cascade very likely qualifies for the **seasonal amusement/recreational overtime exemption** under both Wisconsin DWD 274.04(8) and the federal FLSA, so time-and-a-half after 40 hours should **not** be assumed.

### Cited Findings
- Cascade is open until 9 p.m. on weekdays and 10 p.m. on weekends, so "it's not uncommon for staffers to work long hours." Peak-season staff is close to 450 employees — [THOR Industries: Behind the scenes at Cascade Mountain](https://www.thorindustries.com/stories/cascade-mountain-ski-resort-family)
- The average seasonal staff is about 350 full- and part-time employees. Many instructors commute from Milwaukee or Chicago and work mainly on weekends and holidays. This page was about 989 days old when searched, so roughly early 2024 — [Snow Season Central: Cascade Mountain](https://www.snowseasoncentral.com/usa/cascade-mountain)
- A former tube park attendant reported "long hours, holidays are mandatory" (2015 review) — [Indeed reviews: Cascade Mountain Ski Resort, Portage](https://www.indeed.com/cmp/Cascade-Mountain-Ski-Resort/reviews?fcountry=US&floc=Portage,+WI)
- Indeed rates Cascade Mountain (Portage) 3.1/5 for pay and benefits, its lowest category, and 4.1/5 for management. A second Indeed page (Cascade Mountain Ski Resort) gives pay and benefits 3.7/5 from only 6 reviews — [Indeed: Cascade Mountain reviews](https://www.indeed.com/cmp/Cascade-Mountain/reviews?fcountry=US&floc=Portage%2C+WI); [Indeed: Cascade Mountain Ski Resort reviews](https://www.indeed.com/cmp/Cascade-Mountain-Ski-Resort/reviews?fcountry=US&floc=Portage,+WI)
- Snowmaker postings expect weekend and night availability — [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/q-Cascade-Mountain-jobs.html)
- Weather: during a warm spell around January 2024, operations manager Evan Walz said the melt was "not great on snow conditions" but brought "an uptick in business," and that Cascade aims to make as much snow as possible when the cold returns — [WKOW: Warmer temperatures boost ski hill business](https://www.wkow.com/news/economy/theyre-enjoying-it-warmer-temperatures-boosts-ski-hill-business/article_706f16e4-be2b-11ee-bf68-231339b5d74f.html)
- The resort's FAQ claims "100% snowmaking coverage of our trails" — [Cascade Mountain FAQs](https://www.cascademountain.com/faqs/)
- Searches turned up no reports of hours being cut at Cascade Mountain in Portage — [search over Indeed and Glassdoor reviews](https://www.indeed.com/cmp/Cascade-Mountain/reviews?fcountry=US&floc=Portage%2C+WI)
- **Wisconsin overtime exemption:** DWD 274.04(8) exempts any employee of an amusement or recreational establishment that operates no more than **7 months in any calendar year**, or whose average receipts for any 6 months of the preceding year were no more than 33⅓% of its average for the other 6 months. Chapter DWD 274 is to be interpreted consistently with the federal FLSA, which has a comparable seasonal amusement/recreation exemption — [Wis. Admin. Code DWD 274.04 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/wisconsin/Wis-Admin-Code-SS-DWD-274-04); [Wisconsin DWD Hours of Work and Overtime FAQ](https://dwd.wisconsin.gov/er/laborstandards/overtimefaq.htm); [Wisconsin Legislature DWD 274 (PDF)](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/code/admin_code/dwd/270_279/274.pdf)

### Inferences
- Cascade operates from about late November to mid-March, roughly 3.5–4 months (see section 6). That is well under 7 months, so it very likely meets the DWD 274.04(8) seasonal test and the parallel federal FLSA §13(a)(3) test. **Overtime at 1.5x is probably not legally required.** Whether Cascade pays it voluntarily is unknown. For conservative estimates, treat all hours as straight time.
- Hours are likely to peak over the Christmas–New Year period, Martin Luther King weekend and Presidents' Day weekend, and on Friday and Saturday nights. They are likely to be lighter on midweek days in January–February and in March. This follows from the weekend and holiday commuter pattern of instructors and the open-until-10 p.m. schedule. It is an inference, not a source statement.
- Because of full snowmaking coverage, a warm spell degrades conditions but has not shut the resort down. The January 2024 WKOW story actually reported more business. Short-term schedule cuts on slow or rainy days cannot be ruled out, but none were documented.
- Scenario assumptions for estimates: 30 h/week (low), 40 h/week (typical full-time), 45–50 h/week (high, peak weeks).

### Gaps
- No J-1-specific data was found on hours: no sponsor job offers listing guaranteed hours, and no student testimonials (Reddit was not accessible in this environment).
- No documentation was found of whether Cascade pays overtime voluntarily, or of any minimum-hours guarantee in J-1 job offers.
- No reports were found on hours after the holidays or in low-snow winters (2023-24 was a notably warm Midwest winter, but nothing specific to Cascade was found).

## 3. Housing and transport

### Takeaway
No source shows that Cascade Mountain provides or arranges employee housing for J-1 students. The only Cascade-specific source (Snow Season Central, about early 2024) says short-term housing in Portage is scarce and that staff without a car struggle. It points to weekly or monthly rentals in Wisconsin Dells for those with a car and license. Wisconsin Dells has a large network of J-1 dorms built for the summer season, priced around **$85/week** in older reports. Regular apartments run about **$895/month or more**. No resort shuttle was documented.

### Cited Findings

If an employee has a car and driver's license, they may be able to find housing in Wisconsin Dells where they can pay by the week or month." Most staff live within about 50 miles. A share of a $895+/month studio or apartment would be about $100–$225/week per person, depending on occupancy.
- Planning range for a 13–15-week season: **$1,100–$3,000 total for housing**, with the low end only if dorm-style beds are available.
- Transport is likely a real cost. With no documented shuttle, students may need rides, carpools or rideshare, or a housing provider's van. Commuting from the Dells to Portage was not measured in this research.

- No information was found on a resort employee shuttle or public transport between the Dells, Portage and Cascade.
- No data was found on housing deposits, or on whether rent is taken out of the paycheck.

## 4. Taxes withheld from J-1 workers in Wisconsin, and expected refunds

### Takeaway
A Brazilian J-1 Work and Travel participant is a nonresident alien for tax purposes. They are **exempt from FICA** (Social Security 6.2% + Medicare 1.45% = 7.65%), but federal income tax and Wisconsin state income tax are withheld. Nonresidents cannot claim the federal standard deduction (except Indian students). Wisconsin's 2025 rate starts at 3.5%, and a nonresident must file Form 1NPR if Wisconsin-source income is $2,000 or more. Federal refunds are likely to be modest but real, because payroll withholding treats short-season pay as if it ran all year. A meaningful part of Wisconsin withholding may be refundable, but this needs checking.

### Cited Findings
- A J-1 alien who is a nonresident alien for tax purposes is exempt from US Social Security and Medicare taxes on wages for authorized services. The exemption covers services performed "to carry out the purpose for which you were admitted." It does not apply if the person becomes a resident alien for tax purposes — [IRS: Foreign student liability for Social Security and Medicare taxes](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-student-liability-for-social-security-and-medicare-taxes); [IRS Publication 519 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p519)
- If FICA is withheld in error, the worker should ask the employer to refund it. Failing that, the worker can file Form 843 with a copy of Form DS-2019 attached — [IRS: Foreign student liability for Social Security and Medicare taxes](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-student-liability-for-social-security-and-medicare-taxes); [IRS Publication 519](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p519)
- Nonresident aliens cannot claim the standard deduction. Students and business apprentices from India are the exception, under Article 21 of the US–India treaty. Most nonresidents must itemize on Schedule A of Form 1040-NR. A return must be filed to claim a refund of over-withheld tax — [IRS: Nonresident — Figuring your tax](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/nonresident-figuring-your-tax); [IRS Publication 519](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p519)
- For withholding, employers add an extra amount to a nonresident alien's wages (the "withholding adjustment" in Pub. 15-T, per Notice 1392) because the tables assume a standard deduction that nonresidents cannot take. The added amount does not appear on Form W-2. The 2026 amounts per pay period are in the 2026 Pub. 15-T — [IRS Publication 15 (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15); [IRS Publication 15-T (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15t); [IRS Publication 515 (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p515); [IRS Notice 1392 (PDF)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/n1392.pdf)
- **Wisconsin filing:** a nonresident whose 2025 Wisconsin-source gross income is $2,000 or more must file Form 1NPR. Nonresidents with Wisconsin tax withheld can file 1NPR to claim a refund even when not required to file — [Wisconsin DOR 2025 Form 1NPR Instructions (PDF)](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/TaxForms2025/2025-Form1NPR-Inst.pdf); [Wisconsin DOR FAQ: Part-Year and Nonresidents](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/Pages/FAQS/pcs-npr.aspx); [Wisconsin DOR Publication 122 (PDF)](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/DOR%20Publications/pb122.pdf)
- **Wisconsin 2025 rates (single filers, after 2025 Wisconsin Act 15, retroactive to January 1, 2025):** 3.5% up to $14,680; 4.4% from $14,680 to $50,480; 5.3% from $50,480 to about $323,290; 7.65% above that. Some sites still show the pre-Act 15 4.4% range ($14,320–$28,640) — [EY Tax News: Wisconsin middle-income tax cuts](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-1727-wisconsin-law-provides-middle-income-tax-cuts-and-retirement-income-tax-relief-retroactive-to-january-1-2025); [TaxAct](https://blog.taxact.com/what-is-wisconsin-state-income-tax/); [ustax.tools](https://ustax.tools/wisconsin-tax-brackets-2025/)

### Inferences
- **FICA saving:** at $16/hour × 40 h × 13 weeks = $8,320 gross, the exemption saves about **$636** that a US worker would pay. Students should check pay stubs for erroneous "SS/Medicare" deductions; payroll mistakes with J-1 workers are common.
- **Federal income tax:** with no standard deduction and, to my knowledge, no US–Brazil income tax treaty (not verified in this research), nearly all wages are taxable. A season gross of $5,000–$13,000 falls in the 10% federal bracket. Final liability is therefore about **10% of gross**, which is about $830 on $8,320. The exact 2025 and 2026 bracket thresholds were not verified here.
- **Over-withholding and refund (federal):** payroll withholding uses the percentage method, which annualizes each paycheck. For example, $640/week × 52 = $33,280 a year, so part of each check is withheld at 12% as if the student earned that all year. The nonresident add-on in Pub. 15-T pushes withholding higher still. Because the student actually earns only about $8k, total withholding is likely to exceed the final 10% liability, giving a **federal refund on the order of a few hundred dollars**. This is an estimate; it depends on the 2026 Pub. 15-T tables.
- **Calendar split:** a December–March season spans two US tax years. December wages fall in one year and January–March wages in the next, which keeps each year's income low and may require two federal and two Wisconsin returns.
- **Wisconsin:** at 3.5% on roughly $8,320, gross Wisconsin tax is at most about $290 before any deduction or exemption. Form 1NPR prorates Wisconsin's standard deduction and exemption by the ratio of Wisconsin income to federal income. For a J-1 student whose entire US income is from Wisconsin, that ratio is about 100%, so Wisconsin liability could be close to zero and much of the withholding refundable. This reading of 1NPR needs checking against the 2025 1NPR instructions.
- Rough total withholding on a typical paycheck: about 10–13% federal plus 3–4% Wisconsin. Expect about **13–17% of gross withheld**, of which perhaps a quarter to a half comes back as refunds after filing 1040-NR and 1NPR (estimate).

### Gaps
- The 2026 Pub. 15-T nonresident add-on amounts per pay period were not retrieved.
- No Wisconsin withholding table (Wisconsin W-204 / Pub W-166) was retrieved, and no rule specific to nonresident aliens was found for Wisconsin withholding.
- The absence of a US–Brazil income tax treaty was not confirmed from an IRS source in this research. Check the IRS tax treaties A–Z list.
- No actual Cascade pay stubs or J-1 refund reports were found.
- No information was found on whether Cascade deducts anything other than taxes, such as uniforms, meals, housing or parking.

## 6. Season dates, J-1 sponsors and Brazilian agencies

### Takeaway

The 2026-27 season is projected at about Nov 28 – Mar 14, which is an estimate. ### Inferences
- "Summer Work Travel" is the J-1 category that Southern Hemisphere students, Brazilians included, use during their summer break, which falls in the US winter. Snow Season Central's wording therefore fits a Brazilian student working there from December to March.
- A Brazilian student arriving in early to mid December and leaving in mid-March would get about **12–14 working weeks**. Students who arrive before the late-November opening could add 1–2 weeks. The November orientation may require early arrival, or a separate onboarding for J-1 students (unconfirmed).
- The Intrax link is about two years old. Brazilian agencies, including Travelmate if it places students there, would typically work through a US sponsor such as Intrax. This is plausible but not confirmed.
